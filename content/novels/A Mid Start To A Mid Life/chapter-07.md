**"Mr. Xu."**

**"Yes, I'm here."**

**"Please go inside when the last Daoist leaves."**

**"Sure. Thank you."**

I waited outside the office.

The HR department was located in one of the buildings on Finance Peak.

Calling it a "building" was generous.

Like most structures in the sect, it was carved directly into the mountain.

Unlike most structures in the sect, however, it looked absolutely miserable.

Sterile stone walls.

White surfaces.

Simple furniture.

No decorations.

No plants.

No paintings.

Nothing.

The place looked almost exactly opposite to the usual grandeur of the rest of the peak.

Apparently, even cultivators didn't like HR.

And whoever designed this place had apparently decided that the best way to reflect that feeling was to make the entire department look like a prison.

Bleak.

Cold.

Efficient.

Basically, the work ethic of the average HR employee had somehow become an architectural style.

Eventually, the last Daoist walked out.

The receptionist looked at me.

"Mr. Xu?"

"Yes."

"You can go in now."

"Thank you."

I entered.

The reason I was here was simple.

The war had gotten worse.

Much worse.

The Orthodox Alliance had underestimated the enemy.

The Crimson Devils—or, more accurately, the forces supporting them—had managed to breach several of the Golden Talisman Sect's preliminary defenses.

And now...

They had actually managed to encircle the Golden Talisman Sect.

That changed things.

Quite a lot, actually.

War was doing what war always did.

Economic pressure was increasing.

Supplies were becoming more difficult to move.

And, most importantly, the losses on the front lines had become absolutely massive.

The sects of the Orthodox Alliance were starting to run out of people who were willing—or suitable—to fight.

Which meant they were beginning to look elsewhere.

Non-combat cultivators.

People like me.

Mostly volunteers, at least for now.

I really didn't want to deal with that shit.

So I called my sister.

She gave me some advice.

> "Request training in some kind of craft from HR and donate your products. Volunteer your craft to the sect. They'll keep you inside the sect, but you'll have to help produce whatever supplies they need."

It was actually a pretty good idea.

I knew some armor and weapon crafting.

I'd learned the basics on the side during my induction training, back when I was accepted into my current position.

Surprisingly, crafting didn't have that high a barrier to entry.

You needed enough qi.

A Fire attribute helped.

And you needed a ridiculous amount of free time.

The basic principle was honestly pretty simple.

Use your qi to manipulate the material.

It was basically playing with extremely expensive spiritual playdough.

With enough practice, you could reach around the third tier of crafting before talent started becoming a serious bottleneck.

Third-tier crafting would be more than enough for what I needed.

I didn't need to become some legendary artifact refiner.

I just needed to avoid getting sent to the front lines.

I reached the desk.

The Daoist behind it smiled.

"How can I help you, fellow Daoist Xu?"

"Yes. I'd like to contribute through my craft."

Her smile immediately became brighter.

"That's wonderful, fellow Daoist Xu!"

She pulled out several documents.

"Please take a look at this one first. You'll need to sign here, and here, and..."

I looked down at the paperwork.

There were a lot of signatures.

A suspiciously large number of signatures.

I had a feeling "voluntary contribution" was a very flexible term.

Still.

I signed.

---

A while later, I left the HR "building."

Or mountain.

Whatever.

The greenery outside looked almost absurdly peaceful.

Trees swayed gently in the wind.

Birds chirped.

Spiritual flowers bloomed along the paths.

It was difficult to believe there was a war going on.

I stood there for a moment.

Would the war really end this simply?

Right now, almost nothing had changed for ordinary people inside the sect.

Prices were still controlled by the sect.

Most of the things we bought were still subsidized.

Food.

Cultivation resources.

Transportation.

Basic necessities.

Everything was still more or less normal.

For now.

The war had only gone from "distant problem" to "inconveniently close problem."

But if things continued getting worse...

Would everything eventually go to shit?

"Hmm..."

Maybe I should invest in something.

Or stockpile some resources.

Maybe I should talk to the guys and see if we could start a business.

War had always been profitable for some people, right?

Maybe we could be those people.

Not that I wanted to profit from people's suffering.

Obviously.

I just wanted to profit from...

Supply shortages.

Logistics.

Increased demand.

Other perfectly respectable economic phenomena.

Completely different.

...

"Yeah."

I really needed to talk to someone with more experience.

Someone who actually understood economics in this world.

Because my current financial strategy was basically:

_Save money._

_Buy cultivation resources._

_Don't die._

Not exactly sophisticated.

Then I remembered something.

"Oh, yeah."

I should probably ask what Placeholder wants to do for her contribution.

And whether anyone had messaged her.  
or hit on her...

...

Yeah.

Way too nosy.

I definitely got that trait from Mom.
